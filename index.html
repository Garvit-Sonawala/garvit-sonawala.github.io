About

I'm a fifth-year BS–MS student in Physics at IISER Thiruvananthapuram, and I'm pursuing a BS in Data Science from IIT Madras alongside it.

Over the past few years I've worked across a handful of genuinely different problems: dark matter freeze-out in the early Universe, gravitational-wave signals buried in pulsar-timing data, and now, for my Master's thesis under Dr. Rusa Mandal at IIT Gandhinagar, a rare B-meson decay compared against LHCb data. None of these were planned out as steps toward one fixed topic, I followed what seemed interesting at the time. What ties them together, looking back, isn't really a subject so much as an approach: the theory is what pulls me in first, working out how a system actually behaves from first principles, and then building or using whatever computational tool it takes to turn that into a real, testable number against the data.

I like the theoretical side of things most, and the computational skills work alongside that rather than apart from it, both aimed at the same goal: actually understanding a system, not just producing an answer faster. It's the same instinct behind MindSort, the app I built and published this year, which has nothing to do with physics: you tell it what's on your mind and it sorts your work into projects, works out what's outstanding, and each morning suggests what to actually focus on, so instead of a flat to-do list you get real clarity on where you stand across everything you're juggling. More on that below.

MAY 2026 – NOW

Master's Thesis, IIT Gandhinagar

Now

Supervisor: Dr. Rusa Mandal

Computing the rare decay B → K*ℓ⁺ℓ⁻ from first principles: matching the one-loop Standard Model amplitude onto the effective weak Hamiltonian, then comparing the result with LHCb data to bound new-physics contributions to the underlying Wilson coefficients. Started as an SRIP fellowship at IIT Gandhinagar, May to July 2026.

JAN – MAY 2026

Minor Project, Computational Chemistry

IISER Thiruvananthapuram · Supervisor: Dr. R. S. Swathi

Used particle-swarm optimisation, benchmarked first on the classical Lennard-Jones cluster problem, to find global-minimum structures of (B12)n borophene nanoclusters (n = 2 to 10), treating each 12-atom monomer as a rigid body to cut the search space from 36n down to 6(n−1) dimensions. Pairing a ring communication topology with L-BFGS-B local refinement got the swarm to 100% success up to n = 8, well past where a plain swarm had already collapsed. Comparing the full potential against a version without its antiaromatic correction isolated that term's contribution at 10–20 kJ/mol, and the results picked out n = 6 as an anomalously stable "magic number" cluster, matching an independent reference calculation.

MAY – JUL 2025

Student Intern, TIFR Mumbai

Supervisor: Dr. Achamveedu Gopakumar

Simulated twelve years of pulsar-timing-array residuals for circular supermassive black-hole binaries at 0PN and 2PN order, using real timing data from three pulsars with injected Gaussian noise. Built a 1D CNN to tell genuine signal from noise, which held around 98% accuracy at high signal-to-noise and degraded gracefully as it dropped, and an Elman RNN to regress chirp mass directly from the residuals, getting mean relative error down to around 2.5% at high SNR. Bayesian neural networks added calibrated uncertainty on top of those estimates.

JUL – SEP 2024

Research Intern, IIT Guwahati

Supervisor: Dr. Subhaditya Bhattacharya

Started from the observational case for dark matter (rotation curves, gravitational lensing, the Bullet Cluster, CMB anisotropies), then derived the relic-abundance equation from first principles: the Boltzmann equation on an FLRW background, reduced from Liouville's theorem down to the standard yield equation that governs thermal freeze-out in the radiation-dominated epoch.

Building

I like automating things that are repetitive and don't really need a human's judgement, usually with some machine learning or computation doing the actual thinking. Model a system well enough and you don't have to do the boring part by hand anymore. Physics, basically, pointed somewhere else.

MindSort

Voice & text personal-assistant PWA

MindSort is a Progressive Web App I designed and built end to end. You "dump" whatever's on your mind, typed or spoken, in English, Hindi, or a mix of both, and it works out what you actually meant: a new task, something you've finished, a reason to reshape today's plan, or just you thinking out loud. Nothing gets written until you confirm it. Every morning it puts together a short, reasoned daily briefing from your real open tasks and behavioural signals it's picked up over time, things like which projects have gone quiet or which ones keep losing their slot, instead of just sorting everything by due date.

Under the hood it's a React PWA on Supabase Postgres, with row-level security on every table, voice transcription through Deepgram, and both AI providers called through server-side proxy functions so no API key ever reaches the browser. The model proposes changes, but a deterministic layer in the code re-verifies anything that actually matters, so it can't silently invent a due date or a finished task.
